How it works

From the text you paste to the verdict on screen, in five steps.

1

Input

You paste a headline, a paragraph or a full article on the Analyze page.

2

Clean

Source fingerprints are removed: Reuters datelines, “Featured image via…”, links, @handles. The model judges the writing, not the publisher.

3

Tokenise

BERT's WordPiece tokenizer splits the text. Long articles become overlapping 256-token chunks (50% overlap).

4

Classify

The fine-tuned BERT model gives each chunk a FAKE/REAL probability; chunk scores are averaged.

5

Explain

Confidence ≥ 65% shows Real or Fake; below that shows Uncertain. Very short inputs get a warning.

What the model is good at

  • Spotting sensational, emotional, clickbait-style writing
  • Recognising neutral, attributed reporting (“officials said on Tuesday”)
  • Full articles and paragraphs: ~99% accuracy on unseen test articles
  • Inputs of any length, from a headline to a long article

Limitations

  • It does not check facts: a false claim written calmly can look “real”
  • Trained mostly on 2016–17 US political news, so other regions and topics are less reliable
  • One-line statements carry little style information
  • High confidence means “reads like”, not “is true”
Tip for best results: paste at least one full paragraph (50+ words) of the article. For important claims, always confirm with trusted news outlets or fact-checking sites.
